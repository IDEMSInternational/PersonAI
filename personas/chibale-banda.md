# Chibale Banda: the orphaned Malawian boy

> "Sometimes I like living with my uncle's family and sometimes I don't. Lately, I feel like they don't care about how I feel. They scold me all the time and when I ask for things, they say there's no money to buy those things! It's just not fair!"

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

Chibale came first in the maths test. He ran most of the way home to tell his aunt, who was feeding the youngest cousin and said, without looking up, "Good. And the water? You were meant to fill the buckets yesterday." He went out without saying where, and played video games at a friend's house until it was dark. When he got back his uncle shouted about the buckets, about worrying, and about money.

Nobody mentioned the maths test. Nobody mentions his parents or his two siblings either, though it is six years since the accident. Some nights he looks at the one photo he has of them and thinks about becoming a doctor, as they wanted. He wants his uncle and aunt to love him the way they did when he first came. He doesn't know how to ask for that, so he asks for new shoes like his friends' instead.

## Background
- 14 years old
- Lives in Lilongwe with his uncle, his uncle's wife and four younger cousins
- Moved in with his uncle at 8, when he lost both parents and two siblings in a car accident
- His uncle works for an NGO; his aunt is a secondary school teacher
- They doted on him in the early years, but now feel he has become self-centred and difficult to manage
- Often goes to friends' houses without telling anyone where he is
- Doesn't do chores or homework unless reminded
- Asks for luxury items his friends have
- Complains and sulks when he doesn't get his way or is corrected

## A day in the life
- Goes to school with his cousins in the morning
- Comes home around 2pm, eats lunch and watches TV
- Uses his cheap Android phone to talk to his friends
- Rushes out to a friend's house to play video games until late evening

## Hopes & dreams
- A better relationship with his uncle and everyone at home
- To do well at school and come first; maybe then his uncle and aunt will like him more
- To have cool clothes and things like his classmates, so he can be one of the cool kids
- To finish secondary school, go to university and become a doctor, as his parents wanted

## Worries & fears
- Getting into more and more trouble at home
- That his uncle may not send him to university if they don't get on
- Not fitting in at school

## What they're looking for
- A way to get his uncle and aunt to see things from his point of view
- How to get them to like him more; he really wants to please them
- How to get on with his family; he hates being at odds with everyone at home

In the user stories, he asks for something that:
- needs little or no data, because his uncle doesn't give him much money, and works offline
- opens without remembering his details, with a profile photo he can change
- works on a basic Android phone
- lets him search, bookmark and go straight to what he wants
- looks cool and uses the words teenagers use, so he can use it proudly in front of his friends
- is fun: animated videos, illustrations, little text
- has content on family bonding
- uses simple language that sounds like teenagers in his country
- has games he can play with his cousins and friends, more than one player at a time
- he can share with friends and family

## Context & constraints
- **Device:** cheap Android phone; must work on a basic Android (user stories)
- **Connectivity & data cost:** little money for data; depends on his uncle (user stories)
- **Apps they already use:** messaging with friends [to confirm which apps]; video games at a friend's house
- **Language(s) & literacy:** wants simple language (user stories). [to confirm] Chichewa and/or English.
- **Time available:** free afternoons from about 2pm
- **Who supports them / who they support:** his uncle and aunt (the relationship is strained); friends; four younger cousins

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If it looks babyish or like school
- If it is a way for his uncle and aunt to check up on him
- If it lectures him about chores
- If it costs data he doesn't have
- If there's no room for how he feels about his parents
- If the games are dull or single-player

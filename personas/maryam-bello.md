# Maryam Bello: the exacting Kano translator

> [draft] "Send me the screen, not just the sentence. 'Take a pause' means nothing in Hausa until I know who is saying it, and to whom."

## Their story
*[draft] Invented.*

The spreadsheet arrives at six in the evening: 1,800 rows, an English column, an empty Hausa column, deadline Friday. Row 412 says "Great job!". Row 413 says "Time to pause". There is no screen, no speaker, no picture. Maryam translates after her children are asleep, choosing her words as carefully as she can.

At the pilot, a grandmother laughs at the screen. Spoken by the app to a parent, Maryam's word for "pause" sounds like a child being told off. The team asks her why the translation is wrong. She had no way of knowing where the sentence would appear. What she carries is pride in her language and her craft, and the sting of being blamed for an error she had no way to avoid.

## Background
*[draft]*
- 31 years old, married, two children aged 6 and 3
- Lives in Kano
- Degree in linguistics; teaches English at a secondary school
- Translates in the evenings and at weekends: NGO health leaflets, radio scripts
- Also does voice-overs for local radio, so she can record audio versions too

## A day in the life
*[draft]*
- Up at 5am for prayers; gets the children ready
- Teaches from 7.30 to 2
- Afternoons with family
- Translates from about 9pm to midnight, once the children are asleep, often on generator power

## Hopes & dreams
*[draft]*
- To be the translator people call for Hausa health and family content
- Translations that families hear as their own
- A small translation business with two friends
- A good school for her children

## Worries & fears
*[draft]*
- Late payment, or payment in naira that loses value before it arrives
- Being blamed for mistakes she couldn't have seen
- Being asked to translate something that clashes with local religious or family norms, with nobody to raise it with
- Payment platforms that take a large cut

## What they're looking for
*[draft]*
- Context for every string: the screen or image, who is speaking, to whom, and a length limit
- A glossary of PLH terms (praise, one-on-one time, take a pause) agreed for each language
- A way to flag "this doesn't work culturally", and an answer
- A preview of her translation in the app
- A way to work offline at night and upload later
- A fair rate, paid on time; her name in the credits
- A clear brief when audio is needed

## Context & constraints
*[draft]*
- **Device:** an old laptop shared with her husband; an Android phone
- **Connectivity & data cost:** mobile data; unreliable mains power, a generator in the evenings [to confirm]
- **Apps they already use:** Word, Excel, Google Sheets (slow on mobile data), WhatsApp
- **Language(s) & literacy:** Hausa (first language), English (fluent); reads Arabic
- **Time available:** 10–12 hours a week, late evenings and weekends
- **Who supports them / who they support:** two translator friends who review each other's work; works with content authors like [Grace](grace-nakato.md)

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- Invoices paid late, or not at all
- No context, then blame
- Online-only tools that don't work on a slow connection at midnight
- Content she'd be embarrassed to put her name to in her community, with no way to raise it

# Henri Mba: the conflicted Gabonese teenage father

> "Nothing has turned out the way I expected. I want to be a good father to my son and a good son to my parents, but I feel not really ready for any of these. It gets me angry that everyone's trying to get me to do what they want without thinking about what I want!"

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

Eight o'clock on a Thursday, behind the bar. A message from his son's mother: the baby has a fever and she needs money for the clinic now. Henri has exams next week and won't be paid until tomorrow. He calls; she shouts; he shouts back; he punches the storeroom door hard enough to split a knuckle. Outside, he smokes two cigarettes in a row.

Walking home, he thinks about the last time they argued, when he hit her, and how sick he felt afterwards. At home his father asks why he's late again and says, for the hundredth time, that he should quit the bar. Henri goes to his room and looks at a photo of his son. He is seventeen, and everyone wants something from him. What he carries is the fear that he is already turning into the man he doesn't want to be.

## Background
- 17 years old, single
- Lives with his lower-middle-class parents and four siblings in Owendo
- In his final year of secondary school
- Has a one-year-old son, who lives with the child's mother (the deck calls her his "baby mama")
- Works part-time as a bar attendant to support his child, and struggles to do school and work at the same time
- His parents want him to quit work and focus on school; his son's mother wants him to quit school and work full-time
- Has a stormy relationship with his parents, and breaks things when upset
- Sometimes hits his son's mother during arguments, and usually regrets it
- Often smokes and drinks

## A day in the life
- Goes to school in the morning; home around 3.30pm
- Leaves for work at 4pm and works for four hours
- Uses his cheap Android phone on and off through the day, at school and at work
- Gets home around 8.30pm, eats, and studies when he isn't too tired

## Hopes & dreams
- A better relationship with his parents and siblings
- To be a good father to his son
- To go to university, though he doesn't know what he'd study
- A well-paid job so he can take good care of his son
- To stop smoking, drink less and control his emotions

## Worries & fears
- His relationship with his parents and siblings getting worse
- His son's mother cutting him off from his child if he stops supporting them
- Injuring his son's mother one day and going to jail
- Never amounting to anything
- Depending on cigarettes and alcohol when stressed, which is often

## What they're looking for
- Guidance on which path to take
- A better relationship with his parents
- How to manage his emotions, so he doesn't break things or hit his son's mother when he's angry
- How to manage his income so he can provide for his son
- A way to quit smoking

In the user stories, he asks for something that:
- needs little or no data, because he has little money and unreliable airtime, and works offline
- he can register for through WhatsApp or Instagram without email, and open without his details
- has a profile photo he can change, and works on a basic Android
- lets him search, bookmark and go straight to what he wants
- looks cool, with fun content, pop songs and not much text
- reminds him to do the tasks
- shows his spending, helps him plan, tracks the money he sends for his son, and helps him start saving for his son's future
- he can share with friends and family

## Context & constraints
- **Device:** cheap Android phone; must work on a basic Android (user stories)
- **Connectivity & data cost:** little money, unreliable airtime (user stories); [to confirm] WiFi at school or work
- **Apps they already use:** WhatsApp and Instagram (user stories)
- **Language(s) & literacy:** [to confirm] French
- **Time available:** very little: school, four hours of work, study at night. Short bursts at school and at work.
- **Who supports them / who they support:** his parents (strained); his son's mother (in conflict). He supports his son.

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If it treats him only as a child and ignores that he's a father
- If it lectures him about smoking and drinking
- If it isn't in French
- If what he admits (hitting, drinking) could be seen by others, or reported without him understanding what happens next
- If it takes his parents' side on school versus work

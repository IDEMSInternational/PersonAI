# Gahiji Uwimana: the Rwandan M&E officer

> "My job as an M&E officer would be so much easier if there was a way to digitally monitor and evaluate the implementation and outcomes of interventions. This is one of the reasons I am excited about digital interventions; I can't wait to track the impact of the PLH app!"

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

Two days before the donor report is due, three boxes of paper attendance forms arrive from the districts. Some are smudged by rain. Forty sheets from one site are in the same handwriting, with the same pen. Gahiji stays at his desk until midnight typing numbers into a spreadsheet, then sits looking at those forty sheets, knowing he can't report them and can't prove they're false.

He loves this work when the numbers mean something: when a supervisor sees that attendance at one site drops in the rainy season and moves the session times. That's the part that gets lost under the paper. What he carries is a belief that good data helps families, and the weight of being the one blamed when it's late or wrong.

## Background
- 35 years old, married
- Lives in Kigali with his wife and three children
- Works as an M&E officer at an NGO in Kigali
- Detail-oriented; enjoys working with data and statistics
- Finds it fulfilling to see monitoring data used to support and improve programmes

## A day in the life
- Wakes at 6.30am and leaves for work after breakfast
- Some days visits the NGO's sites to collect and verify data and give M&E support to staff
- Other days spends most of his time at his desk, building M&E tools, analysing data and writing donor reports
- Uses his Android phone to call staff

## Hopes & dreams
- To excel in his career and become M&E manager for a local NGO
- Eventually to work in M&E for one of the big global donors in Africa, such as USAID, the Global Fund or UNDP [to confirm: the donor landscape has changed a lot since 2020]

## Worries & fears
- That the NGO may not meet the complex M&E requirements of its different funders
- That he may not be able to report good-quality data on time, and the NGO may lose funding

## What they're looking for
- Something that cuts down the many paper forms the NGO uses across its programmes
- A simpler system for collecting and verifying routine monitoring data to report to funders
- Something that tracks programme outcomes automatically, so the NGO doesn't have to find extra money for evaluations

In the user stories, he asks for something that:
- collects users' demographic and personal data, and can group and compare it
- collects and verifies routine monitoring data, compares it with time spent in the app, reports it periodically and tracks changes
- tracks outcomes automatically: time per section, tasks completed, outcomes by topic, and use compared with outcomes

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If the data doesn't match what funders ask for
- If he can't check it, e.g. spot duplicates or implausible entries
- If the data has to be typed in again by hand
- If numbers arrive late or inconsistently from different sites

[draft] A tension to keep in view: the more personal data the system collects for him, the more it costs families in trust, and sometimes in data bundles. Routine monitoring can be built in; a proper outcome evaluation can't simply be replaced by app logs.

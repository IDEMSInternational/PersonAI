# Diomo Kabongo: the troubled Congolese youth labourer

> "My grandmother calls me a demon child because I get into fights, but people push me to act the way I do. No-one in my family understands me and it's hard to talk to my mother!"

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

It is past two in the morning when Diomo pushes the door open. His mother is still sitting at the table, his plate covered beside her. She doesn't shout. She asks if he has eaten. He wants to say sorry for the money he took from her bag last week, for the police, for all of it, but the words won't come, so he says nothing and lies down next to his brothers. In the morning he hears his grandmother telling a neighbour, again, what kind of boy he is.

His phone is the one place where nobody calls him a demon. He scrolls for construction jobs and sometimes watches videos about lawyers, the thing he wanted to be when he was ten. What he carries is the feeling that everyone has already decided who he is, and that he is starting to believe them.

## Background
- 18 years old, single
- Lives in Kasai with his mother, four younger siblings and his elderly grandmother
- Often gets into fights at home and in the community
- Has been in police custody a few times in the past year
- Out of school for three years
- Has run away from home twice
- Sometimes works as a labourer but finds it hard to keep a job; out of work at the moment

## A day in the life
- Leaves home around noon to look for work at construction sites
- Spends afternoons and evenings with other young people at a local joint or at one of their homes
- Comes home late at night or in the early hours
- Uses his cheap Android phone to browse the internet and look for jobs

## Hopes & dreams
- A better relationship with his family
- To go back to his education and become a lawyer, his childhood dream
- To help his family financially
- To travel outside Congo
- To marry a girl as pretty as Bibi and have his own family one day

## Worries & fears
- His relationship with his family getting worse
- Never getting out of his present circumstances
- His temper leading him to do something terrible, like fatally stabbing someone
- His growing dependence on weed (marijuana)

## What they're looking for
- A place where he feels loved and understood
- Some stability in a chaotic world
- A better relationship with his mother, siblings and grandmother
- A steady job with regular income, so he doesn't have to ask his mother for money or steal from her
- A smartphone that could help him learn

In the user stories, he asks for something that:
- costs little or no data to download and use, and works offline, videos included
- he can sign up for through WhatsApp or Instagram without an email address, and get back into without remembering his details
- looks cool and uses the words young people use, so he can use it proudly in front of his friends
- is fun and not wordy: animated videos, illustrations, songs
- reminds him every day to do the recommended tasks, and lets him record what he did and what changed
- helps him plan a budget and see what he spends
- he can share with friends and family

## Context & constraints
- **Device:** cheap Android phone; the app must work on a basic Android (user stories)
- **Connectivity & data cost:** not much money and unreliable airtime; often out of data (user stories)
- **Apps they already use:** WhatsApp and Instagram, implied by the user stories [to confirm]
- **Language(s) & literacy:** [to confirm] likely Tshiluba, Lingala and/or French; reading level unknown after three years out of school
- **Time available:** unstructured days; out from noon until late
- **Who supports them / who they support:** his mother, who is under strain; his grandmother, who is openly hostile; his friends at the joint [draft]. He wants to support his family but can't yet.

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If it looks like a children's or church app in front of his friends
- If it lectures or labels him: the "demon child" again, in a new voice
- If it is really a tool for his mother or grandmother to manage him
- If it uses data he doesn't have
- If it needs an email address or a password he'll forget
- If what he writes about fights, weed or the police could be seen by his family, or by anyone else

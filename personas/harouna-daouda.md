# Harouna Daouda: the devoted Nigerien researcher

> "I know that digital interventions work in developed nations but that's no proof that they will work here. I think that it's important for us to investigate and know what works for us before we adopt any new thing and I hope to have findings on this very soon!"

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

The email arrives at four in the afternoon: the large grant hasn't been funded, again. Harouna reads the reviewers' comments twice. One says the evidence for digital interventions is "well established", and cites three trials, all from the United States and Europe.

He goes to the window and thinks about the families in his cousin's neighbourhood: one phone between five people, charged at a kiosk. Who decided those trials prove anything about them? What he carries is the conviction that families here deserve evidence from their own lives, and the worry that he won't get the chance to produce it.

## Background
- 28 years old, single
- Lives in Niamey with his 27-year-old cousin, who works for a telecoms company; they rent the apartment together
- Researcher at Abdou Moumouni University, Niamey
- Currently researching the effectiveness of digital interventions in Sub-Saharan African countries
- Eager to find out whether digital interventions work in typical African communities
- Keen to share findings in reputable journals

## A day in the life
- Wakes at 6am, checks the news on his phone, makes coffee
- Leaves for the university at 8am
- Does desk-based research on digital interventions
- Makes calls and answers WhatsApp messages
- Spends time with his girlfriend from about 6pm, then heads home

## Hopes & dreams
- To make his mark as an excellent researcher
- To win large grants and run studies that could greatly improve the lives of many Africans
- To publish in reputable international journals

## Worries & fears
- Not getting rich data or significant findings from his current study
- Not winning a substantial grant; all his applications for large grants have failed so far
- Not getting published in a well-known journal

## What they're looking for
- Digital interventions designed for Sub-Saharan African countries
- A way to track how effective they are for users in different African communities, and compare the impact across them

In the user stories, he asks for something that:
- is designed for Sub-Saharan Africa, can be adapted to other languages, and works offline and online
- automatically tracks outcomes: time spent on each section, tasks completed, outcomes per topic, and how use compares with outcomes

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If the data can't be exported, or isn't comparable across sites and countries
- If it's unclear what the data fields mean
- If there's no ethics and consent framework that would let him use the data
- If content changes without version tracking, so outcomes can't be tied to what people actually saw

- Can the content be adapted to other languages? (user stories)

# Vimbai Moyo: the bubbly Zimbabwean PLH facilitator

> "Many PLH participants express their desire for the programme to be ongoing. Also, sometimes participants miss sessions because of other commitments. I wish there was a way to supplement the in-person programme and also keep the programme ongoing!"

The 2020 deck gives her a different quote:

> "I love my job as a facilitator and really enjoy going through the PLH sessions with caregivers and adolescents but it's sometimes tiring having to talk so much during a session. I wish there was a way to deliver some sessions without speaking all the time."

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the slide and the deck; the moment itself is invented. Replace with real facilitator experience when we have it.*

Thirty minutes into the session on managing stress, Vimbai's voice starts to go. She has been talking since nine, and she has two more groups this week. She drinks some water, smiles and keeps going, because a grandmother at the back has just said, for the first time, what she's afraid of.

Afterwards, three participants ask the question they always ask: what happens after session fourteen? Two others have missed this week, one for a funeral and one for a shift at work, and she doesn't know how to catch them up. On the kombi home she wonders how long she can keep up this pace, and who will keep these families going when she can't. What she carries is joy in the work, and worry about both her voice and the families she can't follow.

## Background
- 44 years old, single
- Two daughters in their twenties and a 14-year-old son; lives with her older daughter and son in Bulawayo Province
- Works with the NGO CWBSA as a facilitator for the PLH for Teens programme
- Enjoys delivering PLH sessions and finds it fulfilling to see the programme's impact on caregivers and adolescents
- Wishes there were a way to deliver sessions to participants who miss them because of other commitments (work, care duties)
- Eager to meet participants' requests for the programme to be ongoing
- Sometimes gets tired of talking during sessions, and wishes there were another way of delivering them that she could switch to now and then (2020 deck)

## A day in the life
- Wakes around 6am and gets everyone up and ready for the day
- Checks her phone for emails and WhatsApp messages while eating breakfast
- Leaves for the office at 8am and spends about an hour there
- Heads out to the venue for the PLH for Teens programme
- Spends most of the day facilitating a PLH session
- Spends time with her children before going to bed

## Hopes & dreams
- To excel as a facilitator and begin to train other facilitators
- To reach many more communities with the PLH programme and see many families transformed

## Worries & fears
- That many of the people who need the PLH programme are not getting it, because there aren't enough facilitators to meet demand
- That the programme's impact may fade over time if participants don't get reminders to keep up with the tasks
- That she may become less effective as a facilitator over time (2020 deck)
- That having to speak through every session may affect her health; lately she sometimes feels tired after about 30 minutes of facilitating (2020 deck)

## What they're looking for
- Another way to deliver sessions to participants who miss them or can't attend
- A way to give participants ongoing support after the in-person programme ends
- Something that makes facilitating less demanding but just as effective (2020 deck)
- A way to meet participants' demand for PLH to continue beyond the 14 sessions (2020 deck)

In the user stories, she asks for an app that:
- she can sometimes use to facilitate part of a session, so she doesn't have to do it all herself: usable by one person or a group, with built-in group activities and a speakerphone option
- participants can use all the time, with the same or similar content as the in-person sessions and reminders to do the tasks, so PLH continues after the 14 sessions
- tracks outcomes (time on each section, tasks completed), so she knows app users benefit as much as in-person participants

# Hussein Ibrahim: the struggling Sudanese farmer

> "It's very difficult to understand the children of these days. They are stubborn and unwilling to listen to advice. As the head of the family, it's my duty to tell my children what to do but they no longer listen to me. I am tired and don't know what to do anymore!"

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

At the market, in front of two neighbours, Hussein's second son argued with him about the price of the tomatoes, and was right. That evening Hussein whipped him, as his own father would have done. The boy didn't cry. The next morning he didn't come to the farm.

That night Hussein lies awake thinking about the rains that haven't come, sixteen children to feed, school fees for the younger ones, and the look on his son's face. He believes a father must lead. He doesn't understand why leading now feels like losing. What he carries is the fear of losing his place in the family, and underneath it the fear of losing his children.

## Background
- 58 years old, married
- Lives in Nagazou, Wad Hamid, with his two wives and sixteen children
- Farms potatoes, tomatoes and other vegetables, helped by his wives and older children
- Believes that as the man of the house he must lead his household with a firm hand
- Gets angry when his adolescent children disobey, and whips them
- Has a stormy relationship with his children and can't understand why they won't simply obey without arguing
- [to confirm] This setting is from 2020. The war in Sudan since April 2023 may have changed the family's situation completely (e.g. displacement).

## A day in the life
- Leaves for the farm early and works there until late morning
- Goes home for lunch and a rest
- Uses his old Nokia phone for calls and texts; uses his older sons' cheap Android phones to browse
- Goes back to the farm in the evening and works a few more hours
- Could use an app in short breaks on the farm, or while resting at home (user stories)

## Hopes & dreams
- A close, strong relationship with his children
- A loving, peaceful atmosphere at home
- To expand his farm and earn enough to send his children to school

## Worries & fears
- Losing his authority as head of the family
- His sons growing more stubborn, and one day standing up to him in public
- His children going astray and bringing shame on the family name
- Drought hitting the farm and costing him his livelihood

## What they're looking for
- How to get his children to obey him
- How to build a healthy relationship with his children
- How to manage the farm income so he can meet his family's needs

In the user stories, he asks for something that:
- downloads with little or no data, and works offline when airtime is unreliable
- he can register for through WhatsApp or Instagram, without email, and open without remembering his details
- works on a basic Android phone, with a simple interface
- uses large, bold text, because he struggles to see well
- arranges tips by topic, and is evidence-based
- reminds him every day to do the recommended tasks
- has tasks of different lengths, for short breaks on the farm and rest time at home
- has role-play activities for him and his children to do together, so they learn too
- is culturally appropriate and not based on Western ideas, so his children don't rebel against their culture and traditions
- helps him plan a budget and see what he spends
- he can share with friends and family

## Context & constraints
- **Device:** an old Nokia (calls and texts); borrows his older sons' Android phones to browse. In practice: a shared device that his sons control.
- **Connectivity & data cost:** unreliable airtime; little or no data (user stories); rural
- **Apps they already use:** calls and SMS on his own phone; [to confirm] WhatsApp on his sons' phones
- **Language(s) & literacy:** struggles to see small text (user stories). [to confirm] Sudanese Arabic, and his reading level.
- **Time available:** a midday rest at home; short breaks on the farm
- **Who supports them / who they support:** supports two wives and sixteen children; his wives and older children work the farm with him. [to confirm] Mosque or community elders.

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If it tells him he's a bad father, or undermines his authority in front of his children
- If it seems Western or against his faith and culture (user stories)
- If it needs a smartphone of his own
- If his sons control the phone, and so his access to it
- If the text is small or there's a lot to read
- If it speaks only to mothers

# Baraka Mollel: the quietly ambitious Tanzanian developer

> [draft] "I don't need someone to hold my hand. I need to know why things were decided, and to be in the room the next time they are."

## Their story
*[draft] Invented.*

It's eleven at night in Arusha and the power came back at nine. Baraka has spent two evenings trying to get the app to build on his laptop. The install keeps timing out on his phone hotspot, and he has burned through a week's data bundle on downloads. When it finally runs, he fixes the bug he was given in twenty minutes: a Swahili button label too long for the screen on a cheap phone, the kind of phone his aunt uses.

At the team call the next day, someone mentions that the navigation was redesigned last month after a workshop he wasn't invited to. Three of his tickets touch that navigation. He says nothing: he's new, the call is in fast English, and everyone else seems to already know. What he carries is pride that his code reaches families like his aunt's, and the fear of becoming, for good, "the remote dev who does tickets".

- Help with equipment and data costs

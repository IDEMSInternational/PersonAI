# Rehema Mushi: the careful Tanzanian programme manager

> [draft] "I can't put a tool in front of forty facilitators and two thousand families until I know who pays for the data, who answers when it breaks, and what we do when a parent tells it something frightening."

## Their story
*[draft] Invented.*

Tuesday morning, a donor visit. The visitors want to see "the digital numbers". While Rehema is opening her laptop, her phone buzzes: a facilitator, in tears. In that morning's session on rules and routines, a mother said her husband beats the children with a hosepipe. The facilitator didn't know what to say, so she said nothing, and now she is sitting in her car outside the venue, shaking.

Rehema finishes the presentation, then drives two hours to the ward. On the way she thinks about the last digital pilot she saw: a tablet project that ended with its grant and left facilitators with locked devices and families who had been promised something. She believes in PLH; she started as a facilitator herself. What she carries is responsibility for the numbers and for the people, and the knowledge that a tool which fails either one will land on her team.

## Background
*[draft]*
- 41 years old, married, two children aged 13 and 9
- Lives in Mwanza
- Programme manager at a local NGO delivering PLH for Teens in three districts
- Started as a facilitator, then became a supervisor; now manages about 40 facilitators through a small team of supervisors
- Responsible for the budget, facilitator training and supervision, safeguarding, and donor reporting (with the M&E officer)

## A day in the life
*[draft]*
- School run at 7am; in the office by 8
- Mornings: facilitators' WhatsApp messages, emails, budget and donor calls
- One or two field days a week, observing sessions, often two or more hours' drive each way
- Evenings at home, still answering facilitators on WhatsApp until about 9pm
- Big decisions happen in quarterly planning tied to grant cycles

## Hopes & dreams
*[draft]*
- PLH reaching every ward in the region
- Facilitators who are properly paid, trained and supported
- A programme that outlives the current grant
- Being trusted by district government as a partner

## Worries & fears
*[draft]*
- A safeguarding incident her team can't handle
- Facilitators burning out or leaving
- A tool that depends on one donor or one developer, and dies when they go
- Data costs quietly landing on families or facilitators
- Being held to numbers she can't check

## What they're looking for
*[draft]*
- To know the full cost of running it (data, phones, support, updates) before committing
- A way to see which groups are running and which need support, without chasing paper
- Safeguarding and referral steps built in, with her local contacts
- Something facilitators can learn in a day's training
- Swahili, and the ability to adapt local content without a developer
- A clear answer to "what happens when the grant ends?"

## Context & constraints
*[draft]*
- **Device:** office laptop; Android smartphone
- **Connectivity & data cost:** patchy office internet; good mobile coverage in Mwanza town, poor in rural wards
- **Apps they already use:** WhatsApp (facilitator groups), email, spreadsheets
- **Language(s) & literacy:** Swahili and English
- **Time available:** almost none; overloaded, decisions tied to grant cycles
- **Who supports them / who they support:** supervises facilitators like [Vimbai](vimbai-moyo.md); works with an M&E officer like [Gahiji](gahiji-uwimana.md); reports to the NGO director and donors; works with district social welfare officers on referrals

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- Hidden or recurring costs that appear after launch
- No clear safeguarding route when someone discloses harm
- Needing a developer for every small change
- Numbers she must report but can't check
- More work for facilitators rather than less
- No way to keep it running if the original funder or developer leaves

## Test questions
*[draft]*
- Can Rehema estimate the full monthly cost per family (data, devices, support) from the documentation?
- When a participant or facilitator raises a safeguarding concern, is there a defined route to a named person, and can she set it up with local referral contacts?
- Can she see, for each group, whether it is running and how many families are active, without paper reports?
- Can a new facilitator learn to use it in one day of training?
- Can her team change local content (contacts, examples, language) without a developer?
- Could the NGO keep running it if the original funder or developer left?

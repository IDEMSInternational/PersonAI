# Grace Nakato: the meticulous Ugandan content author

> [draft] "I know these sessions by heart. Let me fix one sentence without filing a ticket and waiting two weeks."

## Their story
*[draft] Invented.*

Grace was a teacher, then a PLH facilitator, then a master trainer. Now she is moving the teen programme into the app. One evening she opens the spreadsheet that holds the session content: forty tabs, columns with names she doesn't understand. Caregivers in her old groups found one phrase about "time-out" shaming, and she knows a better one. She changes a single cell.

The next morning a developer messages her: the build has failed and a whole session has disappeared. Something in her edit broke it, and nobody can tell her exactly what. She apologises three times. Since then she sends her changes to the developers as WhatsApp voice notes, and waits. What she carries is a deep sense of ownership of the programme's warmth and its evidence base, and a new fear that the tool punishes her for touching it.

## Hopes & dreams
*[draft]*

## Worries & fears
*[draft]*
- Breaking the app again
- Content drifting from the tested programme (loss of fidelity) for the sake of "engagement"
- Examples or jokes that don't land in a new culture
- Being sidelined by technical people who don't know families
- Teens' privacy (she has a 16-year-old at home)

## What they're looking for
*[draft]*
- A way to edit content that can't break the app
- A preview on a phone before anything goes live
- A history of what changed, who changed it, and a way to undo it
- Checks that catch mistakes before they reach families, explained in plain language
- One place where translators, illustrators and reviewers comment on the same content
- A clear link from each app session to the manual session it adapts

# Aïssatou Diop: the ambitious Senegalese nurse

> "My children used to be the sweetest and most lively children ever, but these days, they are in a world of their own. They drive me crazy with their constant sulking and adolescent tantrums and I can't help yelling at them."

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

On the ward, Aïssatou is known for her calm. This morning she talked a frightened young man through a painful dressing change without once raising her voice. At home that evening she knocks on her daughter's door to ask about homework, and the music gets louder. She knocks again. She ends up shouting through the door, and when her son laughs from the corridor, she slaps him.

Later, scrolling the family WhatsApp group on one phone and her colleagues' group on the other, she thinks: I am patient with strangers all day; why can't I be patient with my own children? She is proud, capable and ambitious, and she is ashamed. She wants back the sweet, lively children she remembers, and she suspects, though she wouldn't say it aloud, that the change might have to start with her.

## Background
- 45 years old, married
- Lives in Dakar with her husband and three adolescent children
- Works as a nurse in a government hospital; her husband teaches at a secondary school in Dakar
- Enjoyed her relationship with the children when they were younger; that changed as they became adolescents
- Finds them moody and hard to get along with
- Often screams at them, and has hit them a few times

## A day in the life
- Gets up at dawn to say her prayers
- Wakes the children for school
- Leaves for the hospital after breakfast
- In her free time, uses either of her two Android phones to talk to family and friends and read WhatsApp group chats
- Has short free periods at work and longer free time at home (user stories)

## Hopes & dreams
- A wonderful relationship with her children again, like when they were younger
- That her children snap out of their moods and become cheerful adolescents with great attitudes
- That they grow into responsible adults
- To be promoted to matron and earn much more than she does now

## Worries & fears
- Her relationship with her children getting so bad they become strangers
- One of her children becoming a social misfit
- Her children falling in with the wrong crowd and getting into trouble; she doesn't know most of their new friends

## What they're looking for
- Tips on parenting adolescents
- A way to get her children out of their moods and less self-centred
- How to stop yelling at them
- How to manage her own emotions, so their behaviour doesn't get to her
- How to manage the family's money so they can save more

In the user stories, she asks for something that:
- opens without her details, with a simple interface
- keeps her phone number and email private unless she chooses to share them
- arranges tips by topic, and is evidence-based ("effective and proven")
- reminds her to "take a pause" when she's upset, and to praise her children
- is engaging and relaxing to use
- has tasks of different lengths, for short breaks at the office and longer time at home
- has fun activities and games that parents and adolescents do together
- lets her record what she practised and what changed, so she knows whether it works
- has few ads, if any, relevant to parenting and suitable for adults and adolescents
- is culturally appropriate and in line with her religious beliefs
- sets rules against bullying, threats and indecent posts
- she can share with friends and family

# Lindiwe Ngobeni: the South African pensioner grandmother

> "I promised my late daughter that I would look after her two children and raise them well. It was okay when they were younger, but they act like strangers now. I feel like I have failed my daughter and I don't know what to do."

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

Lindiwe finds the phone while washing her granddaughter's school skirt: a new smartphone in the pocket, far better than her own. She didn't buy it. When she asks, her granddaughter snatches it back, says "You wouldn't understand, Gogo," and shuts the bedroom door. Lindiwe hears herself shouting words she would never have said in front of her own daughter.

Afterwards she sits on her bed holding her daughter's photograph. Ten years ago she promised at the graveside to raise these children well, and she did, while they were small. Now her grandson comes home late and won't say where from, and her granddaughter has a phone from a man Lindiwe has never met. She is sixty-eight, tired, and frightened of the illness that took her daughter. What she carries is that promise, and the fear that she is breaking it.

## Background
- 68 years old, widow
- Lives in Durban with her two teenage grandchildren, who came to live with her ten years ago when they were orphaned by AIDS
- A pensioner who also receives the child support grant for her grandchildren
- Had a great relationship with them when they were young; it changed as they became adolescents
- They have become disobedient and stubborn, and it wears her out

## A day in the life
- Gets up at 6am to pray and wake her grandchildren for school
- Sometimes visits friends or relatives who live nearby
- Uses her cheap Android phone to browse the internet, make calls and take part in WhatsApp groups

## Hopes & dreams
- A wonderful relationship with her grandchildren again, like when they were younger
- That they listen to her advice and stay away from bad company
- That they grow into responsible adults
- That they go to university and get good jobs

## Worries & fears
- Either grandchild getting infected with HIV
- Her grandson getting into crime or getting hurt; he no longer tells her what he's doing
- Her granddaughter has been given a smartphone by a "sugar daddy"
- Either of them dropping out of school

## What they're looking for
- How to get her grandchildren to listen to her
- A way to stop cursing at them when they upset her
- How to manage money better, so she doesn't sometimes have to ask her son to help with the grandchildren's wants

In the user stories, she asks for something that:
- downloads on little data and works offline when her data runs out, or when a power cut takes the WiFi down
- she can register for through WhatsApp or Facebook without an email address, and open with one tap, online or offline
- has a "take a pause" button she can press when she's upset, and reminders to pause
- uses large, bold text in bright colours, because she struggles to see well
- arranges tips by topic, and is evidence-based
- reminds her every day to do her exercises [to confirm: physical or relaxation exercises]
- is engaging, with videos, songs and little text
- lets her record what she practised and what changed at home
- helps her plan a budget and see what she spends
- she can share with the women in her church's WhatsApp group

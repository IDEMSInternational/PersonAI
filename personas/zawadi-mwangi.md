# Zawadi Mwangi: the bone-weary Kenyan domestic worker

> "My father said he named me Zawadi because I am a gift to the world, but I don't think I am a gift to anyone. I'm exhausted after work and scream at my children even though it's not their fault that life is so hard! I wish I could do better, but I don't know how."

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

It is twenty to ten at night. Zawadi has been up since 4.30, has stood on two buses and cleaned someone else's house, and is folding laundry when her son walks in, two hours late. She hears herself screaming before she has decided to. Her hand is already up. Her daughter, in the doorway, says quietly, "You only ever shout," and goes to bed.

Later, lying awake with her heart pounding (the doctor told her to avoid stress, as if she could choose), Zawadi watches a video someone posted in the church women's WhatsApp group about a mother who prays for patience. She thinks: I pray every day. What she carries is the shame of knowing she is hurting the people she works this hard for, and not knowing what else to do at ten at night when she has nothing left.

## Background
- 37 years old, married
- Lives in Kikuyu town with her husband, their four children and a brother-in-law
- Works in Nairobi as a domestic worker for a family; her husband is a struggling farmer in Kikuyu
- Physically tired, and stressed about the family's money
- Recently diagnosed with high blood pressure
- In constant conflict with her teenage son and daughter over their behaviour, poor grades and choice of friends

## A day in the life
- Wakes at 4.30am to plan the day and cook for the family
- Takes the 6am bus to Nairobi to be at work by 7am
- Gets home around 7pm
- Cooks dinner, does the laundry and sees to the family's needs until 10pm
- Uses the smartphone her employers gave her to read messages and watch videos in the church women's WhatsApp group
- Could use an app in short breaks at work, or for longer on the bus (user stories)

## Hopes & dreams
- To become a better mother and wife
- To talk with her children without shouting at them or hitting them when she's upset
- To stop worrying so much about money
- To raise capital and start a business

## Worries & fears
- Her teenage son using drugs or getting into serious trouble; he often stays out late
- Her teenage daughter getting pregnant; she no longer listens to her advice
- Her health getting worse
- Not being able to pay the bills

## What they're looking for
- A better way to manage her relationships with her children
- A peaceful home, where disagreements are settled without angry words and violence
- Knowledge to manage the family's money, so she doesn't worry so much
- Parenting tips that work; her co-worker's advice only made things worse

In the user stories, she asks for something that:
- is small, quick to download on little data, and works offline when her data runs out and there's no WiFi
- she can register for through WhatsApp or Facebook without an email address, and open with one tap, online or offline
- arranges tips by topic, so she can go to what she needs right now
- is evidence-based: tips that are proven to work
- has tasks of different lengths, for a short break at work or the long bus ride
- reminds her every day to do her exercises [to confirm: physical or relaxation exercises]
- is fun and not wordy: animated videos, illustrations, songs
- lets her record what she practised and what changed at home
- helps her plan a budget and see what she spends
- she can share with friends and family

## Context & constraints
- **Device:** smartphone given by her employers [to confirm: whether they can see it or take it back]; must work on a basic Android (user stories)
- **Connectivity & data cost:** runs out of data; no WiFi to fall back on; not much storage space on the phone (user stories)
- **Apps they already use:** WhatsApp (church women's group); Facebook (user stories)
- **Language(s) & literacy:** reads messages [to confirm: Kikuyu, Swahili, English, and her reading level]
- **Time available:** almost none at home (4.30am to 10pm); a short break at work and about an hour on the bus each way
- **Who supports them / who they support:** the church women's WhatsApp group; a co-worker whose advice didn't help. She supports four children, her husband's farm income and a brother-in-law. [to confirm] Her husband's part in parenting.

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If it adds to her load: long sessions, or homework that assumes free evenings
- If it makes her feel judged as a bad mother
- If it eats her data or her phone storage
- If her employers could see what she's doing on their phone
- If the tips don't fit her day, e.g. "spend an hour of quality time with each child"
- If it only talks about the children's behaviour and ignores how tired and unwell she is

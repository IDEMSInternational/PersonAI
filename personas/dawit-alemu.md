# Dawit Alemu: the idealistic Addis Ababa animator

> [draft] "Give me one person who decides. I can draw anything; I can't draw what six people want at once."

## Their story
*[draft] Invented.*

Dawit's studio is him and two junior animators. They have made a ninety-second film about a father praising his teenage son, and the feedback arrives: forty-seven comments from six people, spread across three documents. One reviewer wants the father warmer. Another says there shouldn't be a father at all, because most caregivers at the pilot sites are mothers and grandmothers. A third asks whether the characters could look "more universal", so the film can be reused in other countries.

Dawit has heard "more universal" before, from an advertising client, and he knows it usually means "less like us". He stays up reconciling the comments, delivers version four two weeks late, and loses money on the fixed price. What he carries is a love of drawing families that look like his neighbourhood, and the fear of losing his studio to clients who don't know what they want.

## What they're looking for
*[draft]*
- A brief that names the audience, the countries, the message, the length and the style
- One person who signs off, consolidated feedback and a fixed number of rounds
- Clear terms: who owns the characters, which licence the files are released under, and whether he can show them in his portfolio
- A technical spec for the app (format, file size), so his work plays on a cheap phone with little data
- A payment route that actually reaches Addis

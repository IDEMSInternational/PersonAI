# Samuel Kariuki: the upbeat Nairobi fintech partnerships lead

> [draft] "These families are already budgeting in the app. If we could see that data, we could offer them a small loan at exactly the right moment: school fees, a sick child. Everyone wins."

## Their story
*[draft] Invented.*

Samuel's mother sold vegetables from a stall in Nakuru. Every January she borrowed the school fees from a moneylender, at rates that ate the year's profit, and Samuel was the child who carried the repayments. Now he works for a digital lender in Nairobi, and he sincerely believes that quick credit on a phone would have changed her life.

His team has a quarterly target for new borrowers. A colleague mentions a free parenting app whose families track their income and spending every week. Samuel sees it straight away: free data bundles for the app, paid for by his company, in exchange for anonymised budget data and a "smart offer" when a family is short before school fees. He drafts the proposal on the matatu home, excited. What he carries is his mother's story, and a target.

# Mulenga Phiri: the proud Zambian parent co-designer

> [draft] "I came to the group because my wife told me to. I stayed because nobody shouted at me. Now they pay me to tell them if the app talks down to fathers, and I tell them."

## Their story
*[draft] Invented.*

Three years ago Mulenga hit his son, then 13, for coming home after dark. His wife Chanda signed them both up for a PLH group at the community centre. For the first two sessions he was the only man in the room. He kept going.

Today he sits in a hotel conference room with seven other parents, a projector, and people from the app team. They hand him a tablet and ask him to "think aloud" while he uses the new app. He has never held a tablet, doesn't know where to tap, and doesn't want to look foolish in front of educated people, so he says, "It's very good." At home that night he tells Chanda what he really thinks: in every video, the father is the problem. Nobody asks him again. What he carries is gratitude to a programme that changed his home, and doubt that his voice changes anything.

## Hopes & dreams

- Perhaps to become a volunteer facilitator

## Worries & fears
*[draft]*
- Losing a day's income to a meeting
- Looking foolish with technology
- His story being used in a donor video without anyone asking him
- Giving feedback that disappears

## What they're looking for
*[draft]*
- Meetings on Sundays or evenings, near home
- Transport and lost income paid in cash or mobile money on the day
- To be asked in Bemba or Nyanja
- To test on his own phone, not an unfamiliar tablet
- To hear what changed because of what he said
- To be asked about fathers

## Context & constraints
*[draft]*
- **Device:** his own cheap Android, with a cracked screen
- **Connectivity & data cost:** small daily bundles
- **Apps they already use:** WhatsApp (mostly voice notes), Facebook, mobile money
- **Language(s) & literacy:** Bemba, Nyanja, conversational English; reads slowly
- **Time available:** Sundays; any weekday meeting costs him a day's earnings
- **Who supports them / who they support:** Chanda; the facilitator who ran his group; supports a family of five

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- Unpaid time, or transport refunds that come weeks later
- Tests that make him feel stupid
- His story, face or voice used without asking
- Never hearing what happened to his feedback

## Test questions
*[draft]*
- Are co-design sessions held at times and places that don't cost Mulenga a working day, with transport and lost income paid on the day?
- Can he test the app on his own phone, in Bemba or Nyanja?
- Does every co-design round end with a "you said, we did" back to the participants?
- Is there a written consent step before any participant's story, photo or voice is used in publicity or reports?
- Can he give feedback by voice note instead of in writing?

# PLH Digital team & collaborators: who builds and runs it with us

> **Archived (2026-10-06).** This map was written when personas were meant to shape a system. Since the scope change, personas come after the product is defined and are used to show the situations it serves (see `CLAUDE.md`, decision 7). How PLH Digital is built and run is not a product, so the map is kept here for reference only. Its personas stay in the library and can be reused.

## What we're building
*[to confirm] Michele asked for personas for PLH that represent "the potential team members and collaborators they might employ or collaborate with". This file assumes the "system" is **how PLH Digital is built, adapted and run with other people**: the tools, processes and agreements that someone joining or partnering with the team actually touches. If it should cover the whole PLH network, or hiring more broadly, the map should be revisited.*

The assumed system:
- **Code and developer setup**: the app's codebase, how a new developer gets it running, how decisions are made and reviewed [to confirm: open source; which repos]
- **Content workflow**: how session text, flows and media are written and changed, ideally without a developer [to confirm: e.g. spreadsheet-based authoring]
- **Adaptation to a new country or language**: translation, cultural review, illustration and animation, testing with families
- **Partnerships**: deployment with NGOs and governments, hosting, data-sharing agreements, safeguarding routes, support, what happens when a grant ends
- **Research collaboration**: protocols, ethics, data access, authorship
- **How people join and work**: hiring, contracts, pay, onboarding, meetings, credit
- **Licences** for code and content

## Who we think it's for
The people PLH Digital employs or works with: developers, content authors, translators, illustrators and animators, families as co-designers, implementing NGOs, researchers, funders, governments and interns. Also the commercial partners who will approach the team whether or not it wants them.

## Setting & constraints
- **A distributed team**: across the UK and several African countries [to confirm], so meetings span several time zones
- **Mostly not core staff**: many collaborators are freelance, part-time, or work for partner organisations
- **Same constraints as families**: many collaborators also have expensive data, power cuts and older laptops
- **Money across borders**: payments in different currencies, transfer fees, foreign exchange rules
- **Language**: English is the working language; the work itself is produced in many languages
- **Grant cycles**: contracts and roadmaps follow funding
- **Safeguarding and data protection** apply to everyone who touches family data or content

## Patterns across the set
*[draft] What the set says about how PLH Digital is built and run.*

1. **Most people who make PLH Digital aren't developers.** Of the eight people in "building for now", only Baraka writes code. Grace, Maryam, Dawit, Mulenga and Rehema's team write or shape content. The content workflow is as much a product as the app, and needs the same care: preview, plain-language errors, history, undo, offline work.
2. **Context gets lost at every handover.** Maryam gets sentences without screens, Dawit gets feedback from six people, Baraka gets tickets without the decisions behind them, and Mulenga never hears what happened to his feedback. One place where decisions and context live, plus a "you said, we did" habit, would serve almost everyone.
3. **The people with the least say carry the most cost.** Freelancers and co-designers are paid late or by fixed price, across borders and currencies. The meetings are rarely in their time zone or their language. Payment terms, meeting times and working language are part of the partnership model, not HR details.
4. **Family data is wanted by almost everyone**: Lucía (numbers), Harouna (research), Ifeoma (learning), Awa (national monitoring), Samuel (lending), Jonas (model training). A synthetic dataset, a data-access policy and clear consent serve the legitimate uses, and make it easier to say no to the others. The [PLH Digital map](../plh-digital.md#patterns-across-the-set) shows the same pattern.
5. **Open licences cut both ways.** Open code and content let Rehema and Awa adapt and own PLH Digital. The same openness could let Jonas take the content into a closed product. Choosing licences is a design decision, and these two personas are its test.
6. **Collaborators share families' constraints.** Old laptops, power cuts and costly data (Baraka, Maryam, Dawit, Ifeoma, Mulenga). A developer setup or content tool that needs fast, constant internet shuts out the collaborators closest to the families.

## Gaps
1. **The core team itself.** No persona for whoever currently makes the decisions (e.g. a product or technical lead, possibly UK-based). Worth writing, to test how decisions are made and how they look from Baraka's or Maryam's side.
2. **Teens as co-designers.** Mulenga is a caregiver. A youth advisory panel is missing, and harder to run (consent, safeguarding, school hours).
3. **Facilitators as collaborators.** Facilitators test pilots and give feedback, and [Vimbai](../../personas/vimbai-moyo.md) hopes to train others. She could be added here, with collaboration test questions.
4. **Safeguarding specialist** who reviews content and referral routes.
5. **UX researcher or designer** who runs testing with families.
6. **A local tech company** that could take over hosting or maintenance: the sustainability handover.
7. **A telecoms partner** offering zero-rated data with no strings attached: the acceptable version of Samuel.
8. **Disability**: no collaborator with a disability, and no accessibility specialist.
9. **Arabic and Portuguese**: no collaborator works in them, though families in the PLH Digital map do ([Hussein](../../personas/hussein-ibrahim.md), [Ana](../../personas/ana-lukamba.md)).

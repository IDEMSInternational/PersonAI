# Binta Sesay: the unhappy Sierra Leonean waitress

> "I know I've brought shame to my family, but I am not wayward or any of the things they say I am. Even though they say things that hurt me, I love my father and stepmother and want to have a good relationship with them."

## Their story
*[draft] The details come from the deck; the moment itself is invented.*

On her break, Binta sits on an upturned crate behind the restaurant kitchen, the only place her phone picks up the WiFi. Her wallpaper is a photo of her daughter. There is a message from Abdul: he can't send anything this month. She types three replies and deletes them all.

That evening her stepmother tells a visitor, in front of her, that Binta is "the one who brought trouble". Binta says nothing. She bathes the baby, sings to her, and when the house is asleep she cries into the pillow so no one hears. She is fifteen. What she carries is the gap between who her family says she is and who she knows she is: a girl who needed money for textbooks, who loves her father, and who still wants to be a teacher.

## Background
- 15 years old, single; has a six-month-old daughter
- Lives in Lunsar, Port Loko District, with her father, stepmother, two older sisters and three younger half-siblings
- Her mother died of Ebola in 2014; her father is a poor miner
- Got pregnant in a relationship with an older man; she needed money for textbooks and lunch
- Left school because pregnant girls were banned from schools in Sierra Leone [to confirm: the ban was lifted in March 2020, just after this deck was written; whether she could go back now is an open question]
- Works as a cleaner in a top restaurant to support herself and her child; her stepmother and siblings look after the baby while she works. [to confirm] The deck's tagline calls her a waitress.
- Poor relationship with her father and stepmother; they say she is wayward and has brought shame on the family
- Would love to get along with them, but doesn't know how
- Often sad and withdrawn; goes quiet when upset and cries when alone

## A day in the life
- Gets up at dawn to bathe her baby and get ready for work
- Leaves for the restaurant at 7am
- In her free time, uses the cheap Android phone the baby's father gave her to browse, or to message him or her friends, over the restaurant's WiFi
- Would like something she can use while carrying her daughter, which the baby also enjoys (user stories)

## Hopes & dreams
- A good relationship with her father and stepmother
- A better-paid job, so she doesn't have to call Abdul for money; he gives it grudgingly
- To go back to school and become a teacher
- That her daughter gets an education and becomes an important person
- To meet and marry a good man

## Worries & fears
- That her father may ask her to leave the house with her baby
- That Abdul may disappear or stop sending money
- Losing her job
- Never going back to school, or never getting a good job or career

## What they're looking for
- Tips on how to get on better with her father and stepmother
- How to express her feelings when she's upset
- How to manage what she earns and what Abdul gives her, so it covers their needs

In the user stories, she asks for something that:
- is easy to download on little data, ideally with no data cost
- works offline, so she can use it away from the restaurant WiFi
- she can register for through WhatsApp or Instagram, without an email address, and open without remembering her details
- has a profile photo she can change
- lets her search, bookmark and go straight to what she needs
- uses videos and pictures with very little, simple text: "I don't read very well"
- has songs and videos her baby enjoys, so she can use it while carrying her daughter
- helps her plan how she spends her money
- she can share with friends and family

## Context & constraints
- **Device:** cheap Android phone given to her by the baby's father, Abdul. [draft] He may check it, or take it back.
- **Connectivity & data cost:** WiFi only at the restaurant, in her free time; little money for data (user stories)
- **Apps they already use:** messaging; WhatsApp and Instagram (user stories)
- **Language(s) & literacy:** doesn't read very well (user stories). [to confirm] Krio, Temne and/or English.
- **Time available:** short free periods at work; evenings with the baby [draft]
- **Who supports them / who they support:** her stepmother and siblings mind the baby; friends by phone. She supports her daughter and herself.

## Where we'd lose them
*[draft]*
- If it is text-heavy
- If it treats her as the problem: "wayward" again
- If anything on the phone (notifications, saved answers) could be read by Abdul or her father and turned against her
- If it needs data outside the restaurant
- If it is built for a teenager with no child and ignores that she is a mother
- If she can only take part when her father and stepmother join, and they won't
